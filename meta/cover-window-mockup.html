Din · display option mockup, take two

The cover gets its own window

A cover panel inside the queue window cost 336 pt and left one playlist row standing. So: no panel. A 28 pt thumbnail tucked into the empty top-right corner of the titlebar — where it displaces nothing at all — and clicking it collapses the whole window into a square of pure artwork. Move the mouse over it and the close button, the track and the transport fade up over the sleeve; move away and they're gone again. Click the thumbnail below to try it.

Try it

One window, two shapes. Click the thumbnail to collapse, hover the cover to raise the controls, and the × to go back. The waveform is live in both — same seek, drag and hover-preview as WaveformView.swift.

  • Why it's one window and not two

    The window is already .hiddenTitleBar, so there's no chrome to fight: collapsing to a square is a single setFrame(_:display:animate:), anchored at the top-left corner the way macOS resizes anyway. A second window would mean a second lifecycle to keep alive, a second position to remember, and shouldHandleTransportKey's window === mainWindow test would start failing — space, [ and ] would go dead in cover mode.

  • What the thumbnail displaces

    Nothing. The titlebar's right end is the one region of the window that has always been empty — 28 pt tall, traffic lights at the far side, dead space in between. A 28 pt square parked there leaves the title, the transport, the volume button and the waveform exactly where they are today, so a track with no cover doesn't shuffle the layout.

    The cost: that strip is also the window's drag region, and a control punches a hole in it. 28 pt of a 336 pt bar, in the corner furthest from where people grab a window — acceptable, but it is a real trade.

  • The overlay is a scrim, not a bar

    Two gradients — one down from the top for the close button, one up from the bottom for the track, transport and waveform — both fading to nothing by the middle of the cover. At rest you see the sleeve edge to edge with no furniture on it at all.

  • Dragging it around

    With no titlebar and no overlay showing there's nothing left to grab, so the cover needs isMovableByWindowBackground = true — drag it by the artwork itself.

  • Restore the size, keep the position

    Stash the queue size on the way in and hand it back on the way out — but not the origin. If you dragged the square to another corner of the screen, the window should come back where you left it, not teleport home. So: keep the current top-left, restore the old size. macOS frames are bottom-left origin, so that means origin.y -= newHeight - oldHeight — the classic way to get this wrong is to set the size and watch the window grow downward off the bottom of the screen.

  • Why 220 at the bottom

    The hover overlay needs about 130 pt for the title, transport, waveform and times. Below roughly 220 pt square the overlay stops being a scrim over a cover and becomes the whole window — the sleeve disappears behind its own controls. That's the floor, and it's derived rather than picked.

  • Why 420 at the top

    This is a judgement call, not a derivation. The square is a thing you park in a corner and glance at; past about 420 pt it stops being furniture and starts being a lightbox, and on a laptop screen it's eating half the height. Worth trying 480 before settling.

  • The first collapse borrows the width

    With no remembered square yet, the cover opens at the queue window's own width, clamped — so a default 336 pt window gives a 336 pt square and the collapse is purely vertical. Widen the queue past 420 first and you'll see the window narrow on the way in.

When there's no artwork

Plenty of files carry no embedded cover, so the square has to be worth opening anyway — otherwise the thumbnail has to vanish for those tracks, and a playlist that mixes both starts flickering. Three candidates, all at rest, all 336 pt.

What it would take

Smaller than the panel version, and it touches less.

  • The artwork itself

    Unchanged from the panel sketch: one more commonKey case in MetadataLoader, and a @Published var currentArtwork on PlaylistManager loaded beside waveformPeaks — never on Track, which is Hashable and drives a List.

  • A new CoverView, and reuse below it

    The cover's bottom overlay is the top half of ControlsView with a gradient behind it. Worth pulling the transport row out into its own small view so the two can't drift apart.

  • AppDelegate does the collapsing

    It already owns mainWindow and hands out showMainWindow(); a setCoverMode(_:) beside it keeps all the window poking in one place — save the size, lock the aspect, clamp, hide the standard window buttons, and reverse all four on the way out.

  • Two sizes to remember

    din.queueSize so the window comes back exactly as you left it, and din.coverSide so a resized square stays resized — both alongside din.repeat. Sizes, not frames: the position is whatever the window currently has.

  • Clearing the aspect lock

    contentAspectRatio and contentResizeIncrements are mutually exclusive — setting either clears the other. So the way out of the square is contentResizeIncrements = NSSize(width: 1, height: 1), not assigning a zero ratio. Set contentMinSize and contentMaxSize on the way in, and put them back to the queue's 300 × 400 minimum on the way out.

  • A menu item and a shortcut

    The thumbnail can't be the only way in — it isn't there when a track has no embedded art. A View-menu toggle (⌘J?) alongside the existing Din Window item gives it a keyboard route and a name.

  • The tick can slow down

    Same free win as before: while the cover overlay is hidden nothing on screen moves, so ControlsView's 1/30 s TimelineView tick can idle at 1 s until the pointer arrives.

Worth deciding before any code

  • Is × the right glyph

    It sits where the red traffic light sits, so it will read as close the window — but it means go back to the queue. The alternative is the same corner-arrows glyph as the thumbnail, pointing inward. Less familiar, but it says what actually happens, and then the real close button can stay a real close button.

  • Placeholder, or no thumbnail at all

    If a track with no cover still has something to show, the thumbnail never disappears and cover mode never has to snap back to the queue mid-playlist — the nastiest edge case here just stops existing. That's the strongest argument for drawing Din's waves rather than a note glyph, and it's an argument about behaviour, not decoration.

    The counter-argument: a generated square pretends there's a cover when there isn't. If that bothers you, hide the thumbnail for those tracks and decide separately what cover mode does when the playlist walks into one.

  • Should it float above other windows

    A cover square you park in a corner wants to stay visible, which argues for .floating window level while collapsed. That's a real preference, not a default — always-on-top surprises people who didn't ask for it.

  • Hover isn't the only pointer state

    The overlay appears on hover, which means it's invisible to anyone driving by keyboard. If the window can be focused, the controls should raise on focus too — the mockup does this, so tab into the collapsed cover and watch it come up.
